EV Charger AC/DC 800W

Off-board Electric Vehicle Charger

Project Description

  • An 800 W off-board AC/DC charger for electric vehicles, based on a PFC stage and a digitally controlled half-bridge LLC converter, designed for high efficiency, safety and low weight.
  • It supports multiple battery types and charging modes, and is controlled by the vehicle over CAN.
  • Role Firmware Developer
  • Period May 2026 - Aug 2026
  • Team size 3

Key Features

  • Charging modes: pre-charge, constant current (CC), constant voltage (CV) and constant power (CP)
  • Support for multiple battery types
  • Charging power and limits controlled by the vehicle through CAN commands
  • Digital closed-loop control of the LLC power stage
  • Firmware update over CAN with recovery from interrupted updates

My Responsibilities

  • Analyzed customer requirements and contributed to the software architecture
  • Developed embedded firmware in C for Renesas RL78 and RX66T microcontrollers
  • Contributed to the design and development of the PID control loops and the CC/CV/CP charging control algorithms
  • Developed UART communication between the two microcontrollers
  • Developed ADC signal acquisition with digital filtering to reduce measurement noise
  • Implemented calibration data management in data flash to correct measured values
  • Designed and developed a firmware update over CAN that can recover and re-run the update if a previous update was interrupted
  • Performed firmware integration, hardware bring-up and system debugging
  • Created and executed unit, integration and system tests, and worked with hardware engineers to validate the charger and support product release

Technical Challenges & Solutions

  • Safe firmware update in the field: a power loss or communication error during an update could leave the charger unusable. I designed the update over CAN so that an interrupted update is detected and the charger can always recover and re-run the update.
  • Accurate measurements in a noisy power environment: switching noise from the power stage distorted the ADC readings, and component tolerances caused offset and gain errors between units. I added digital filtering to the ADC acquisition and applied per-unit calibration data stored in data flash, giving the control loops stable and accurate feedback values.
